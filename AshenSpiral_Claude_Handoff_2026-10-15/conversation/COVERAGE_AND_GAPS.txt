CONVERSATION COVERAGE

What is preserved verbatim
- The human messages still visible in the active chat context, transcribed in
  AVAILABLE_CHAT_MESSAGES.txt. Their wording is kept exactly, including shorthand.
- Original uploaded 0.22, 0.23 and 0.23.1 prompt ZIPs and their supplied media.
- Uploaded 0.20 delta text, bugfix PDF, earlier request notes, build logs and
  screenshots. Their original bytes are kept in prompts_and_inputs.
- Earlier request/design/provenance documents present in the delivered projects.

What is summarized
- The available continuation context describing work before this export, including
  the partial source changes, decisions, unresolved integration, known constraints,
  and intended next steps. This is organized in handoff/ and CONTEXT_SUMMARY.txt.
- Relevant earlier Ashen Spiral context returned by conversation retrieval, in
  RECOVERED_PRIOR_CONTEXT.txt. Those entries are retrieval summaries, NOT quotes.
- Most historical game discussion before the visible messages is represented by
  project documents, catalog changes and the summaries rather than full chat turns.

What is unavailable
- A complete verbatim transcript of this entire long chat, including earlier turns
  omitted from current context, has not been exposed to the exporting assistant.
- Full past message timestamps, any unseen attachments and any decisions never
  stored in the accessible context/files cannot be reconstructed exactly.
- No invented dialogue or claims that unavailable chat history was exported.

How to fill the gap if exact wording becomes necessary
The user can supply a ChatGPT conversation/account export or copied missing turns
to the receiving agent. This archive is sufficient to inspect the actual source,
read the original current briefs, compare versions and continue the known work;
it is not a substitute for a complete platform chat export.

This package includes all accessible game-related inputs identified in this
workspace and all known current prompts. It excludes account credentials, private
file-transfer tooling, generated build/cache directories and unrelated personal
context. Original build logs may retain the local paths supplied by the user.
